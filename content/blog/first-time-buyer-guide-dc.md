## 1. Get Pre-Approved Before You Tour

This is non-negotiable in the DMV. Sellers and their agents expect a pre-approval letter with every offer. Going through the pre-approval process also helps you understand exactly what you can afford and flags any credit issues early.

## 2. Understand the DC, MD, and VA Tax Differences

Each jurisdiction has different transfer and recordation taxes. DC buyers pay a combined rate of around 2.2% of the purchase price, Maryland varies by county, and Virginia's rates are generally lower. Your settlement attorney will explain the specifics for your transaction.

## 3. First-Time Buyer Programs Are Valuable — Use Them

- **DC:** The DC Open Doors program offers 0% interest down payment assistance up to 3.5% of the purchase price.
- **Maryland:** MMP (Maryland Mortgage Program) offers below-market rates and down payment assistance.
- **Virginia:** VHDA offers down payment assistance and reduced mortgage insurance.

Ask your lender about all programs you qualify for.

## 4. Budget for Closing Costs

On top of your down payment, plan for closing costs of 2–4% of the purchase price. In a competitive market, sellers are less likely to offer concessions, so have this cash ready.

## 5. The Importance of the Home Inspection

Never waive a home inspection for a DMV property. Even in a hot market, you should understand what you're buying. Work with a certified inspector who knows the region's housing stock — older DC rowhouses and newer suburban builds have very different issues to watch for.

## 6. Know Your Commute Options

The DMV is a Metro-centric market. Proximity to Metro stations significantly affects both price and liquidity (how easily you can sell later). If you don't own a car, plan your home search around Metro access. If you drive, understand peak traffic patterns before committing to a neighborhood.

## 7. HOA and Condo Fees Are Real Costs

Many condos and planned communities have monthly fees ranging from $200 to $800+. These affect your purchasing power. Make sure your lender includes them in your debt-to-income calculations.

## 8. Multiple Offer Situations Are Common

In competitive neighborhoods, you'll likely face multiple offers. Work with your agent to understand escalation clauses, appraisal contingency waivers (and the risks), and what makes your offer competitive beyond price.

## 9. Think About Your Timeline to Sell

Most first-time buyers in the DMV plan to move in 5–7 years. Choose a property that will appeal to future buyers — consider school districts, walkability, and neighborhood trajectory.

## 10. Build Your Team Early

You'll need a real estate agent, lender, settlement attorney, and home inspector. The best professionals in the DMV are often booked weeks out. Start building your team before you're ready to make offers.
